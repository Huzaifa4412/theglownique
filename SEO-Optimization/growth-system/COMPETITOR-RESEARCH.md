## Why the top pages satisfy the query

- **Configurator or instant price** (BuySignLetters, SignMonkey) — the commercial channel-letter results reward buying online.
- **Depth plus named real projects** (SignFreaks #1 for "front lit vs halo lit", ~2.7k words, Chicago projects) — though the AI Overview cited fresher, answer-first pages instead.
- **Precise definitions with component caveats** (Custom Neon's outdoor help article, paraphrased almost clause by clause by the AI Overview: IP67 defined, dimmer not weatherproof, choose outdoor at order time).
- **Quick-answer blocks with numbers** (NeonChamp's wedding size guide) — cited for its inch ranges.
- **Tables** — the AI Overview rebuilds comparison and price tables from pages that have them.

## Where every competitor is weak

- **No primary sources** on any of the 20 decision pages audited; no method notes; author credentials mostly absent.
- **Price ranges disagree by up to 10×**, and installed-project prices are mixed with shipped-letter prices on channel-letter cost pages.
- **Outdoor/IP**: the #1 result never explains IP ratings.
- **Sizing**: guidance is wedding-only; nothing for storefronts, receptions or bars.
- **Terminology**: halo-lit vs backlit is used inconsistently even within the top results.

## Buyer objections (forums, PAA, AI Overviews)

1. Price opacity — "from $16" listings vs $250–$500+ real quotes.
2. Cheap LED quality — visible dots, fading, failing power supplies.
3. Indoor signs bought for outdoor use; power supplies and dimmers are not waterproof.
4. **Who installs channel letters** — licensing, permits, installers' reluctance to mount other companies' products. The core objection for a ship-only seller.
5. Permits and landlord sign criteria.
6. Lead time vs wedding/event dates.
7. Turning a detailed logo into neon.
8. Sizing and readability distance.
9. Running cost, heat, 24/7 use.
10. Lifespan and repairability.
